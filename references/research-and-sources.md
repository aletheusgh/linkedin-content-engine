# Research and Sources

Use external research when a draft makes factual claims beyond the user's verified experience or when scientific/professional validation is requested.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order when applicable:

1. Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, peer-reviewed research, and original datasets.
2. Government, university, standards-body, and intergovernmental publications.
3. Official product, platform, or company documentation for claims about that product or company.
4. Reputable industry research with a disclosed method, sample, date, and limitations.
5. High-quality professional analysis for interpretation, clearly labeled as analysis.

Do not use search-result snippets as evidence. Avoid unsourced blogs, engagement posts, AI-generated summaries, and vendor claims presented as neutral proof.

## Claim workflow

For each material external claim:

1. Define the exact claim before searching.
2. Locate the strongest relevant primary or authoritative source available.
3. Check publication date, population, sample, method, geography, and limitations.
4. Distinguish correlation from causation and individual studies from consensus.
5. Record the source in `.linkedin-content-engine/research/sources.jsonl` with claim, title, organization/authors, publication date, URL or DOI, source type, access date, supported scope, and limitations.
6. Phrase the draft no more strongly than the evidence permits.
7. Place a compact citation near the claim and return a short Sources section with direct links.

Use at least two independent strong sources for consequential or contested claims when practical. One strong primary source can be sufficient for a narrow factual claim. If evidence conflicts, describe the disagreement or remove the claim.

Never label a business recommendation as scientifically validated merely because research is adjacent to it. Separate:

- what the source directly found;
- the interpretation or practical implication;
- the user's own experience or opinion.
